# Creative History  

Historians utilize their methods to explore topics more deeply, spread knowledge to other people, create new ways of understanding the present and the past. This assignment gives you a chance to do that along with a bit of fun. 

For our last assignment, engage in some kind of creative analysis that connects the history of the Inquisition to current social, political, and cultural practices. Can you see inquisitorial procedures or practices at work today? Can you find examples of the use of inquisitorial imagery or rhetoric? Can you analyze the ways that the Inquisition lives on in popular imagination? 

Make something that presents this analysis along with a 300-500 word commentary that explains your project's goals and how you met them.

Potential creations could include, but are not limited to:

1. A text-based adventure game using [Twine](https://twinery.org/) that puts players into the conundrums of prosecutions we've thought about this entire semester. 
2. An interactive map and timeline that moves beyond a list of dates and events, but that helps the audience understand the complexity of interpretation over the years.
3. A design for a historical marker that does more than "complicate" or "commemorate" some aspect of the Inquisition. 
4. A video or written essay on the enduring imaginary of Inquisition or recounting its homology to current social or cultural practices. 
5. A curriculum unit for high schools one some aspect of the Inquisition. 
6. A podcast if you want to be that podcaster type.
7. Some other kind of artistic creation that also makes a historical analysis.
